Why counterbalance the items?

Imagine comparing response times for just these two kinds of trial:
Related primeDOG → CAT
versus
Unrelated primeBUTTON → BREAD
Is any difference due to semantic priming, or because CAT and BREAD are different words? They differ in length and may differ in familiarity or other properties that affect recognition speed.

From item set to two lists

Make two lists so the same target appears in both prime conditions across participants.
List A: prime → condition List B: prime → condition target
DOG → related FOG → unrelated CAT
BUTTON → unrelated BUTTER → related BREAD
Each participant sees one list and each target once. Across participants, CAT and BREAD each appear in both conditions. Gorilla assigns the lists.

Today we only need the principle behind Gorilla’s two spreadsheets. We will work through stimulus design and fuller counterbalancing in Session 5.

Ethics: make your own Teams copy

In Teams → Ethics, make a copy of the template within Teams and add your name to the copy’s title. Edit that copy.
Blue text Reusable lab information; check that it applies to your project.
Yellow text and marked boxes Decide what applies; replace prompts and examples with details of your study.
Appendices Adapt participant materials to match the form and task; remove examples you are not using.
Before submission, delete our teaching cover page, STUDENT CHECK notes, square-bracketed prompts and unused worked examples. Keep the official form wording and your completed answers.

Ethics: where to concentrate

Section 2 Aim, participants, what happens in a trial, conditions, primary outcome, duration, and materials or published measures.
Recruitment, consent and risks Who is eligible, how you invite them, what they agree to, and whether the example answers fit your study.
Appendices Information sheet, consent form, debrief, background questions and representative task materials must match the procedure.
Read every highlighted note and checkbox; do not treat the worked example as a decision already made for your study.
